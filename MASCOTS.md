# Mascots

Each app has a mascot with a name borrowed from a word in another language.

| App | Mascot | Word |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [`hijaiyah/`](hijaiyah) | Sada | Arabic, "echo" |
| [`aksara/`](aksara) | Renjana | Indonesian, "a deep, heartfelt longing" |
| [`logica/`](logica) | Poka | Japanese, from *poka-yoke* |
| [`lexica/`](lexica) | Pochi | Russian, from *pochemuchka* |
| [`numeria/`](numeria) | Myria | Greek, "ten thousand" |

## Sada (Hijaiyah)

Sada is a little lion. The name comes from the Arabic word *ṣadā* (صدى), meaning "echo". Just like an echo, Sada repeats every letter sound back to you, so you can listen, say it, and hear it again until it feels easy.

## Renjana (Aksara)

Renjana is an Indonesian word for a strong feeling of the heart: a deep passion, a longing for something you love. For Renjana, that something is stories. Renjana is the little reader who cannot wait to turn the next page, and who cheers you on as the letters turn into words and the words into stories.

## Poka (Logica)

Poka is a little beaver in a hard hat, and the name is short for *poka-yoke* (ポカヨケ), a Japanese idea meaning "mistake-proofing": designing things so that a mistake is noticed and fixed before it causes trouble. Poka the beaver does the same, like a builder checking each step. It follows your program step by step, so you can see where a mistake hides, and then fix it.

## Pochi (Lexica)

Pochi is short for *pochemuchka* (почемучка), a Russian word for a person who keeps asking "why?" (*почему*). Pochi is a little parrot, curious about every word: what it means, how it sounds, and where it comes from.

## Myria (Numeria)

Myria comes from the Greek word *myrias* (μυριάς), which means "ten thousand", the biggest number the ancient Greeks had a single word for. It later gave English the word "myriad", meaning too many to count. Myria is the friendly owl who loves numbers of every size, from counting apples in the kitchen to working out discounts in the shop.

Only Poka and Myria are named on screen so far. The mascots of the other apps are described here for when they are named in the apps.

Each app's home screen shows a one-line note under the subtitle (for example "Sada · Arabic for “echo” ›"). Tapping it, or the mascot, opens a bottom sheet (text only, no picture) with a shorter version of the text above, written in the mascot's own voice. Those texts live in [`shared/mascot.js`](shared/mascot.js).
