# Omnia

General knowledge for children, in English, with Pacha the llama. The topics are about places, flags, nature, culture, animals, volcanoes, islands, peoples, landmarks and foods, rivers and lakes, the solar system, the water cycle, food chains, and day, night and the seasons, and there are five games on the map (a jigsaw, Who Am I?, Neighbours, Bigger or Farther? and Island Routes). Most of it is on maps a child can drag, pinch and zoom (the solar system is on a made-up map of space); the science topics are animated scenes instead.

A round has six questions (fewer when a level has fewer places), mixing three kinds:

- **Find it**: "Where is Bandung?" The child taps the dot, or for a country its shape or its capital's dot.
- **Which is it?**: one place is lit up on the map; the child picks its name from four.
- **Capital**: "What is the capital of Jawa Barat?" or "What is the capital of Kenya?", four answers, with the place lit up on the map.

- **Flags**: "Which country has this flag?" (four names), "Which one is the flag of Norway?" (four flags) and "Which country has this flag? Tap it on the map."
- **Nature**: "Where is Mount Everest?" (tap the dot), "Which river is this?" (a dot is lit up) and "Which continent is Lake Victoria in?" (not asked for oceans and seas, or for places that are not on one continent, like Puncak Jaya). A river's dot marks one spot along it, a desert's or a sea's dot its middle.
- **Culture**: "Where is Kecak from?" (tap the dot), "Which dance is from here?" (a dot is lit up) and "Which province is Kecak from?" (the other provinces come from the same part of Indonesia first). Gamelan, Tifa and Batik belong to more than one province, so they are not asked for one.
- **Animals**: "Where does the lion live?" (tap a dot where it lives), "Which animal lives here?", "Which continent does the lion live on?" (not for animals that live on more than one, or in the sea) and, in All animals, "Where does the lion live?" with a choice of habitats.
- **Volcanoes and disasters**: "Where is Mount Bromo?" (tap the dot), "Which volcano is this?" and "Which province is Mount Bromo in?". A tsunami's dot is at sea, where its earthquake was.
- **Islands and straits**: "Where is Bali?", "Which island is this?" and, for a strait, "Which two places does the Sunda Strait lie between?". Islands and seas are not asked for a place.
- **Peoples**: "Where do the Batak live?", "Which people live here?", "Which province do the Batak come from?" and "Which language do the Batak speak?". The Javanese, who live in several provinces, and the Dayak, who speak many languages, are not asked for what they have no single answer for.

Flags that are too alike (Indonesia and Monaco; Romania, Chad and Andorra) are never offered together. Flags are emoji, so a device without flag emoji (some Windows browsers) shows two letters instead.

A wrong answer gets a second try, and a wrong tap says which place was tapped instead ("That is Malang"). A second miss shows the place on the map and a sentence to remember. Every level is open from the start.

🔎 **Explore the map** on each topic shows every place: tap a dot or a country to see its name, flag, capital and continent, its province, or what a mountain, river, lake, desert, ocean or sea is.

Where a country names more than one capital, the official one is used (Sucre for Bolivia, Pretoria for South Africa, Sri Jayawardenepura Kotte for Sri Lanka, Gitega for Burundi). Jakarta is Indonesia's capital, and Nusantara is shown as the new capital being built.

- **Landmarks**: "Where is the Eiffel Tower?" (tap the dot), "Which landmark is this?" and "Which country is the Eiffel Tower in?".
- **Foods**: "Where does sushi come from?", "Which dish comes from here?" and "Which country does sushi come from?".
- **The solar system**: "Where is Mars? Tap it." (the dots are not named, so the child has to know the order), "Which planet is this?" and "Which planet is fourth from the Sun?" (not lit up, so it is not given away). Bigger planets and Farther from the Sun use the same two-choice game as Bigger or Farther?, with the diameter and the distance from the Sun. In Put in order, three or four planets are named and the child taps them nearest to the Sun first; a dashed line joins the right ones, and a planet that is not in the list, or comes later, is a miss. Who Am I? has a Space level too.

## Games on the map

### Science

The water cycle and food chains have no map. Their Explore card is **Watch**, a scene drawn in `science.js` and moved with anime.js (the Sun's rays turn, vapour rises, rain falls, the river flows, sparks run along the arrows of a food chain). The same scene is shown in a quiz, without its names and numbers, and with the place that is asked about lit up or, in a food chain, left as a "?" that is filled in when it is answered.

The sky is a globe, not a picture: `sky.js` turns the coastlines of the world map back into latitude and longitude (the inverse of the Natural Earth projection in `project()`), places them on a sphere and draws that sphere in 3D every frame, with the Earth's axis tilted 23.4°. The night side is the half the Sun does not reach, bounded by the terminator, which seen from here is half an ellipse. The view turns round the Earth to keep Indonesia in sight (the Sun on the right for morning, on the left for evening, behind the Earth for night). The clock under the globe is the real local time in Indonesia for the spin shown. In the seasons the Earth is drawn at four places round the orbit (seen from a little above), lit on the side facing the Sun, and says which end leans towards it. The Moon is lit on the side facing the Sun, and the disc beside it shows the lit part as the Earth sees it, growing on the right and shrinking on the left.

Questions are ready-made (`science.js`, and `sky.js` for the sky) and every one has four choices:

- **Water cycle**: "Which step of the water cycle is lit up?", a clue to name ("The Sun warms the sea and the water rises into the air as vapour. What is this step called?"), "What comes right after condensation?" and ten facts (what clouds are made of, what water vapour is, whether water is used up).
- **The sky**: "Why do we have day and night?", "The red dot is Indonesia. What part of the day is it there?" (the globe is stopped at that moment, without the clock), "Why do many countries have seasons?", "Which phase of the Moon is this?" (no name on the picture) and facts about the year, the equator, the Moon's light and the tides.
- **Food chains**: "What does the deer eat?", "What eats the rat?", "What if all the rice plants disappeared? Who would soon go hungry?", "Is the shark a producer, a herbivore or a carnivore?" and facts about producers, predators, prey and what the arrows show. Every name is in one chain only, and the wrong choices are things that are not in that chain and that nothing in it eats or is eaten by (sea animals for a land chain, land animals for the sea), so there is never a second right answer.

A wrong answer gets a second try, and a second miss shows the answer and a sentence to remember, as on a map.

Five more topics are games, not places to explore, so they have no Explore card. In most of them the dots are hidden on the map (they are still there for the keyboard); only Island Routes shows them, because the islands are what you tap.

| Game | Levels | How it works |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 🧩 Map Jigsaw | Southeast Asia, Europe, Africa, North America (the well-known countries), South America | "Where does Switzerland belong?" The shape of the country is in a tray under the map. Drag it onto the map: it follows the finger at the size it has on the map now, and is right when it is dropped on its own country or near its middle. A tap on the country (or its dot) also answers it, for a child who cannot drag. |
| 🕵️ Who Am I? | Well-known countries, All countries, Animals, Landmarks, Foods, Nature, Space | Clues come one at a time ("I am a country in Africa", "I am an island country", the first letter of my capital, my flag, my capital), and the last one lights up the place on the map. Choose the name from four. Three clues keep the first try; every clue after that counts as a miss. |
| 🏘️ Neighbours | Europe, Asia, Africa, the Americas | "Which countries share a border with Ireland? Tap them all." Five countries a round. A wrong tap says it is not next to it (a miss); a second one shows all the neighbours. |
| 📏 Bigger or Farther? | Bigger islands, Bigger countries, Farther cities, Farther capitals | Two places are lit up on the map and the child picks the bigger one, or the one farther from Jakarta (which is marked). A right answer shows both numbers ("Kalimantan is about 539,000 km²"). The two are always clearly different (at least 1.2 to 1.4 times). Distances are along the great circle; sizes are the island itself (Kalimantan and Papua: the Indonesian part) or the country's area. With two choices, a first miss makes the other choice right but not on the first try. |
| ⛵ Island Routes | Sumatra, Java and Bali; Nusa Tenggara; All of Indonesia | "Travel from Medan to Jakarta. Tap each island you cross, in order." Five trips a round. Each right island in its place joins a dashed line on the map. An island that is not on the way, or comes later, is a miss; a second miss shows the whole way. The island dots are named for screen readers (the trip names only two cities). |

The neighbours come from `borders.js`, the land borders in the same Natural Earth data as the maps, made by `tools/make-borders.mjs`. A country is only asked when every neighbour on its list is a country of the game, so Israel, Palestine, Kosovo, Western Sahara and the like are never half-listed (Egypt, Jordan, India, China and Serbia are not asked). French Guiana is left out of France's borders, and Spain and the United Kingdom are not asked because of Ceuta, Melilla and Gibraltar.

## The maps

`maps.js` holds the country shapes: the world (Natural Earth projection) and Indonesia with its neighbours (Mercator), drawn from [Natural Earth](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/) data, which is in the public domain, through the `world-atlas` package. It is made by `tools/make-maps.mjs`, which says how to run it; there is no build step for the page itself. Each map keeps its projection's scale and offset, and `project()` in `index.html` uses the same formulas to place cities and capitals from their latitude and longitude, which are written in the page (`CITY_DATA`, `COUNTRY_DATA`, `NATURE_DATA`, `CULTURE_DATA`, `ANIMAL_DATA`, `DISASTER_DATA`, `WATER_DATA`, `PEOPLE_DATA`, `LANDMARK_DATA`, `FOOD_DATA`, `RIVER_DATA`, `ISLAND_AREA`, `COUNTRY_AREA`, `ROUTES`, `SOLAR_DATA`).

The page has one map (`MapView` in `index.html`), moved into the screen that shows it. One finger drags, two fingers pinch, the mouse wheel zooms, and the buttons zoom in, out and back to the whole level. A tap picks the nearest dot within reach of the finger, otherwise the country under it. Dots keep the same size on screen at every zoom. In a quiz the dots are labelled "Place 1", "Place 2", … for screen readers, so the label does not give the answer away; they can be reached with Tab and chosen with Enter, and the arrow keys move the map.

## Stars and stickers

Three stars for every question right on the first try, two for up to two misses, one for finishing. Thirty-eight travel stickers unlock as the total star count grows (357 stars at most). Days in a row are counted as in the other apps.

## Children and parents

Omnia uses the children shared by every Cendekia app (`shared/profile.js`). Each child's stars and days in a row are kept in `localStorage` under `omnia.v1.<child id>` (`best` holds the stars of each level by its id, such as `id.java`, `w.sea`, `f.sea`, `n.river`, `c.dance`, `a.savanna`, `v.java`, `i.strait`, `p.all`, `l.europe`, `fd.asia`, `j.sea`, `q.w1`, `b.europe`, `r.river`, `k.islands`, `t.west`, `s.planets`, `w.steps`, `c.eats` or `d.day`; never rename them). Every finished round goes to the shared log (`Cendekia.round`) and every first miss to the shared list of things that went wrong (`Cendekia.weak`, labelled with the level and the place; flags, culture, animals, volcanoes, islands and peoples levels start with "Flags: ", "Culture: ", "Animals: ", "Volcanoes: ", "Islands: ", "Peoples: ", "Landmarks: ", "Foods: ", "Jigsaw: ", "Who am I: ", "Neighbours: ", "Rivers: ", "Compare: ", "Routes: ", "Space: ", "Water: ", "Food chains: " and "Sky: " so they are not mixed up with the others), so Omnia shows on the [parents' page](../parents/). The daily time limit set there applies here too.

## Sound, motion and offline

Sound effects are made with the Web Audio API; there is no voice. With "reduce motion" turned on in the device settings, all animation is off, and the map jumps instead of gliding.

`sw.js` precaches the page, `maps.js`, `borders.js`, `science.js`, `sky.js`, the icons and the shared scripts. After changing them, bump `CACHE` in `sw.js` so installed copies refresh.
