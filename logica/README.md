# Logica

Logical and computational thinking games for children, with Poka the beaver. Everything is in English, and there is no voice: prompts are shown in Poka's speech bubble, with short sound effects.

## Children and difficulty

Logica uses the children shared by every Cendekia app (`shared/profile.js`): the same child plays here and in the other apps. The chip at the top left of the home screen switches children or adds one; renaming, the picture, the daily time limit and removing a child are also on the [parents' page](../parents/). The daily time limit applies here too: a level or random map cannot start once it is used up.

Each child picks a difficulty, stored per child: the badge at the top right (🌱 Easy, 🌿 Medium or 🌳 Hard) changes it at any time, and a child opening Logica for the first time is asked to choose. Stars are kept for every difficulty, so switching never loses progress.

| Difficulty | Ages | What changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Easy | 4–5 | 10 small robot maps, a repeat block only at the end, three choices, one question fewer per round, six items in the filter, simple number machines, 4×4 pictures, three bulbs and letters moved one step, 3×3 sudokus that grow to 4×4, towers of two to four disks, 5×5 parking lots with four to six cars, pockets up to 9, circuits of one to two switches, gate tables of two switches |
| Medium | 6–7 | 14 robot maps with repeat blocks, a first function and a first if block; the original questions, 5×5 pictures, four bulbs, letters moved one or two steps, 4×4 and 6×6 sudokus, towers of three to five disks, 6×6 parking lots with five to eight cars, pockets up to 20, circuits of up to three switches, gate tables of two or three switches |
| Hard | 8–10 | 10 bigger robot maps with repeat, function and if blocks; four choices, gaps in the middle of patterns, arrows turning by 45 degrees, two machine examples, up to five balls and chained balances, ten items from four colors and shapes in the filter, 6×6 pictures, five bulbs (up to 31) with no running total, letters moved up to three steps and five-letter words, 4×4 and 6×6 sudokus with more gaps, towers of three to six disks with the fewest moves hidden, 6×6 parking lots with six to nine cars and the fewest moves hidden, pockets up to 99 and a number pad, circuits of up to four switches, gate tables of two or three switches |

## Worlds

Every world has its own levels. A level opens once the level before it has at least one star, and each level gives one to three stars.

| World | Skill | What the child does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Robot Path | Algorithms | Builds a program from arrow blocks, then runs it and watches Poka follow it step by step on a grid with rocks, water, gems, yellow tiles and a flag. Later levels add a repeat block (🔁 ×2 to ×9), a repeat-until block (teal 🔁: until Poka is at the 🚩 flag, or, after tapping the 🚩, until 1, 2 or 3 gems are collected, with a 💎 counter under the board), a function (★, built once in its own tray and used again) and an if block (on a yellow tile Poka takes the first arrow, anywhere else the second). A block limit makes the new block necessary. 🎲 Random map makes a new map every time, for practice without stars. |
| Patterns (8 levels) | Pattern recognition | Finds what comes next or what is missing: colors, shapes, repeating units, growing dot counts, turning arrows, and two rules at once. |
| Magic Machine (8 levels) | Abstraction | Watches items go in and out of a machine, works out the rule (change color, change shape, add one, turn, double, two changes, add two, number rules) and picks what comes out next. |
| Balance (6 levels) | Logical reasoning | Reads one to four balances and picks the heaviest or lightest ball, then finds how many small balls balance some big ones. |
| Fix the Code | Debugging | Runs a program with one wrong block, finds the block and fixes it. The bug is chosen at random each time and is checked to make the program fail. |
| Secret Code (7 levels) | Codes and binary | Reads a picture from rows of 0 and 1 and paints one from a code; writes a row as a short code (3 filled, 2 empty: run-length coding); reads a number from light bulbs worth 16, 8, 4, 2 and 1 and lights the bulbs for a number (binary); reads and writes words whose letters moved along the alphabet (a Caesar cipher). Pictures are random and mirrored, like little space invaders. |
| Mini Sudoku (7 levels) | Deduction | Fills the gaps so each shape is once in every row and column, and in every box of the 4×4 (2×2 boxes) and 6×6 (2×3 boxes) grids. Tap a gap, then a shape. Each puzzle is made fresh and checked to have exactly one solution, so it never needs guessing. After two wrong checks, one wrong square is filled in. |
| Who Is It? (7 levels) | Reasoning with clues | Gives each friend the right pet from a list of clues: "Leo does not have the cat", "Sara's pet can fly" (a fact about the pet), "The fish belongs to Mia or Omar", "Neither Rina nor Budi has the turtle". Two to five friends. Each puzzle is made fresh: true clues are added until only one way to hand out the pets fits them all, then any clue that is not needed is dropped. Tapping a clue crosses it out. |
| Sort the Train (7 levels) | Sorting | Swaps two wagons side by side until the train is in order: smallest first, biggest first or ABC, three to seven wagons. The fewest swaps is the number of pairs the wrong way round; a swap of two wagons already in order is wasted and counts as a miss, and after two a pair in the wrong order glows. |
| Tower of Poka (7 levels) | Planning ahead | Moves a tower of disks to the 🏁 peg, or builds the picture shown, one disk at a time: tap a peg to lift its top disk, then tap the peg to put it on. A big disk never sits on a small one (it is refused, not counted as a miss). Levels go from a small tower to a middle-peg goal, messy starts and rebuilding a picture. Each puzzle is made fresh, and a search from the goal gives the fewest moves from every position. A move that leaves the goal further away than before counts as a miss, and after two, the pegs of the best move glow; ↩ Undo takes a move back. |
| Poka's Parking Lot (7 levels) | Planning ahead | Slides cars around a full parking lot to drive the 🚓 police car out of the exit at the bottom. Drag a car along its lane (it stops at the next car and settles on a cell), or tap it and then tap one of the dots that show where it can slide. Any number of free cells is one move; cars only go straight, up and down or left and right. The cars are small top-down drawings: sedans, buses and trucks, and a police car with a white roof and a flashing light bar. The drag is anime.js's draggable through `Cendekia.fx.drag`. Levels grow from four cars on a 5×5 lot to trucks and a crowded 6×6 lot (Easy uses 5×5, Medium and Hard 6×6). Each puzzle is made fresh: cars are placed at random, a search finds every position they can reach and keeps one that needs the number of moves the level asks for, so it always has an answer and the fewest moves is known. A slide that leaves the exit further away than before counts as a miss, and after two, the car to move and its spot glow; ↩ Undo takes a move back. The fewest moves is hidden on Hard. |
| Poka's Maze (7 levels) | Finding a way | Walks Poka home through a maze with the arrow buttons, the arrow keys, or by tapping the next square. Levels grow from 4×4 (Easy), 6×6 (Medium) or 8×8 (Hard) to 6×6, 8×8 and 10×10, and the Shortcuts and Many ways levels have walls knocked down, so there is more than one way and the child looks for the shortest. Each maze is made fresh: it is dug so that every square can be reached, a start and a house are picked the number of steps apart the level asks for, and two searches give the fewest steps from the start and from every square to the house. A wall is refused (Poka bumps it) and is not counted. A step off every shortest route (into a dead end, or onto a longer way) counts as a miss, once however far the child goes on; after two, the next square toward the house glows. ↩ Undo takes a step back, and a trail shows where Poka has been. The fewest steps is hidden on Hard. |
| Poka's Backpack (7 levels) | Variables | Follows a list of steps (put in, add, take, copy) on the pockets of Poka's backpack and says what is in one pocket at the end. Levels: put in and take out, two pockets, and copy (a copy stays a copy when the other pocket changes later). Each puzzle is made fresh: no step takes more than a pocket holds, no pocket overflows, and the pocket asked about really changes. Easy and Medium pick from three or four answers made of common slips (one step too many or too few, the other pocket, a copy the wrong way); Hard uses a number pad. After two wrong answers, a table shows the pockets after every step but the last. Each wrong answer stands for a slip (a Put taken for an Add, a skipped step, the other pocket's number, the start number, a copy the wrong way round, or one or two off) and is counted per level (`slips` in the log); the parents' summary lists the two slips seen most often, at least three times, with something to try. The Repeat level adds a repeat: a card that does the steps inside it (one or two) again and again, a number of times. Easy has two puzzles to read; Medium and Hard have one to read and two to build. When reading, the wrong answers are a repeat done once, one time too many or too few, or only the first step repeated. When building, tap 🔁 Repeat to put a repeat in the tray (the next blocks go inside it until you tap its label again), change how many times with − and +, and tap a block to take it out; the limit is set so that the fewest blocks without a repeat do not fit, so the repeat is what makes it work. Mixed up plays the kinds of puzzle from the levels before, one after another: Easy has one to read (two pockets or copy) and Build it; Medium and Hard have one to read (copy or repeat), one to build (Build it, Swap or a repeat) and always end with a Debug puzzle. In Debug, Poka's program has one wrong step (a number a little off, an Add for a Take, a Put for an Add, or a copy the wrong way round), so it ends with the wrong number; pick the change that fixes it from three (Medium) or four (Hard) changes. Every puzzle is made from a good program and each change offered was run, so exactly one of them works. The wrong changes are slips too (`wrong-step`, a step that was fine, and `wrong-value`, the right step fixed the wrong way) and show on the parents' summary; after two wrong changes a table shows the pockets after each step of the wrong program. Tap a step (or add a block) to hear what it does, and 📖 Steps, on the world screen and in every puzzle, explains Put, Add, Take and Copy with a before and after picture and an everyday example: Put makes the pocket exactly that number, whatever was in it, while Add keeps what is there. In Build it and Swap the child builds the steps: tap fixed blocks (add and take with the numbers on them, or copy from one pocket to another) into a tray, then press ▶ Run and watch the pockets change step by step. Build it must reach the 🎯 number; Swap trades two pockets with the help of a spare one. A search finds the fewest blocks, and a limit of that plus a few spare keeps the puzzle from being brute-forced with one block over and over. A run that ends wrong (or takes more than a pocket holds) is a miss; after two, a table shows the pockets after every step of the run, and each further miss puts the next block of a working program in the tray. |
| Poka's Workshop (6 levels so far) | Logic gates | Follows the wires of a circuit from its switches, through AND, OR and NOT gates, to a lamp. Levels: switch and flip (predict), both keys (AND) and either key (OR), where the child sets the switches to light the lamp, and mixed wires (predict). In predict levels each lamp has its own switches in fixed positions: tap a lamp to say if it shines, then ✓ Check. Light it shows the lamp at once on Easy; on Medium and Hard the lamp waits for ✓ Check, a wrong try shows what the lamp did, and on Hard some puzzles ask to keep the lamp dark and have exactly one working setting. Every circuit is a tree (each switch is used once, so wires never cross) and every puzzle is made fresh and checked: no circuit is stuck on or off and the lamps are a mix of on and off. After two wrong tries the power in the wires is shown. Tap a gate to hear what it does. In Fill the gate and Build a circuit some gates are missing (a ? box): a box with two inputs is an AND or an OR, a box with one input is a NOT or a plain wire. Tap a box to see each gate in turn, then ✓ Check against the table of when the lamp must shine; any choice of gates that matches every row is right. A wrong try marks the rows that do not match, and from the second one Poka fills in a box. Puzzles are made from a hidden circuit and checked: the table is never always on or off, not every choice of gates fits it, and on Hard exactly one does. 📖 Gates (on the world screen and in every puzzle) explains AND, OR, NOT and a plain wire with a picture, an everyday example and a table. |
| Logic Filter (7 levels) | Boolean logic | Picks every item that matches a rule: one property, AND, NOT, OR, BUT NOT, and rules about size. |

The first time a player opens a world, a short animated "How to play" appears; ❓ How on the world screen shows it again.

Hints: after a failed run, 💡 appears in Robot Path. The first hint shows numbered footprints along a working path, the second shows a working program. In Fix the Code, 💡 makes the wrong block glow. Using a hint keeps the stars at two at most.

Robot stars: three for a program with no more blocks than the solution and fewer than two failed runs, one less for each of those missed. Fixing stars: one less for more than one change, and one less for two or more failed runs after the first change. Quiz stars: three with every question right on the first try, two with up to two misses.

Stickers: twelve space stickers unlock as the player's total star count, over all difficulties, grows (`STICKERS` in `index.html`). Days in a row are counted as in the other apps. Each child's progress is kept in `localStorage` under `logika.v3.<child id>`. Logica's own players from before (`logika.v2`, or `logika.v1`) moved once to the shared children: a player with the same name as a child becomes that child, the player who was playing becomes the current child, and the others become new children.

## Map Maker

🛠️ Map Maker, on the home screen and in Robot Path, lets a child build a robot map: pick a size (4×3, 5×4 or 6×5), then tap Poka, the flag, rocks, water, gems (three at most) or grass onto the squares. Poka checks the map with the same search used for the levels and says how many moves it takes, or that the way is blocked, so ▶ Play, 💾 Save and 🔗 Share only work for a map that can be done. Playing a map uses the blocks of the child's difficulty (arrows on easy, arrows and repeat otherwise); its stars are shown but not kept, and "✎ Change the map" goes back to the editor.

Each child keeps up to twelve maps in My maps (`maps` in their saved state), to play, change or delete. 🔗 Share opens the device's share sheet, or copies a link, with the map in the address: `#map=00300-04150-00000-00000` has one digit per square (0 grass, 1 rock, 2 water, 3 gem, 4 Poka, 5 flag) and a dash between rows. Opening the link shows the map in Map Maker, ready to play; a link that is not a map of one of the three sizes, with one Poka, one flag, three gems at most and a way through, is ignored.

## For parents

👪 For parents, at the bottom of the home screen, opens a summary after a small sum for grown-ups (a × b). For each child it shows:

- stars in all, days in a row, puzzles solved this week and the share right on the first try
- a chart of puzzles solved per day over the last 14 days (with a table for screen readers)
- every skill with its stars at the current difficulty and a status: Strong (80% or more right on the first try), Growing (50–79%), Needs practice (below 50%), or Just started (fewer than three tries)
- up to four levels with the most mistakes, worth practicing
- tips, such as moving up a difficulty when most answers are right, or playing a world together
- what each world trains

For the summary, every child keeps `log` (per difficulty, world and level: questions and how many were right on the first try, robot runs, wins, failed runs and hints) and `days` (puzzles solved per day, the last 60 days). This is counted from the first game after the summary was added; earlier stars are included. Nothing leaves the device.

Every finished level also goes to the shared log (`Cendekia.round`), and the first mistake of a question or the first failed run of a level to the shared list of things that went wrong (`Cendekia.weak`), so Logica shows on the [parents' page for all apps](../parents/) with time played, progress, mistakes and last rounds.

## Arc UI components

The star counts roll like an odometer, a toast announces a new sticker or a used hint, ❓ How opens as a draggable bottom sheet, quiz progress is a stepper, and in Sort the Train the wagons are a list to drag (or move with the arrow keys on a wagon's handle), using the real [Arc UI](https://uiarc.dev) `animated-counter`, `toast-stack`, `bottom-sheet`, `stepper` and `reorderable-list` components. A wagon moved past others counts as the side-by-side swaps it takes, so the fewest swaps and wasted swaps mean the same as when tapping (React 19, copied into `ui/src/components/arc/` as Arc intends). Logica itself stays a static page: only the small bundle in `ui-dist/` is built, from `ui/`, and committed.

```bash
cd logica/ui && npm install && npm run build
```

`ui/src/main.tsx` exposes `window.LogicaUI.counter(el, n, suffix)` and `window.LogicaUI.toast({ type, title, description })` and `window.LogicaUI.sheet({ title, html, closeText })`, `window.LogicaUI.progress(el, total, done)` and `window.LogicaUI.reorder(el, { items, hinted, onReorder })`; `index.html` falls back to plain text, the old dialog, pips and tap-to-swap wagons if the bundle is missing. The bundle is not in the page's first load: it is fetched on the first touch or key (and when the difficulty or How to play sheet opens), so it never delays the first paint, and `sw.js` keeps it for offline once fetched. A child's very first visit uses the plain difficulty dialog for the same reason. Arc's tokens are scoped to `.arc-ui` so they do not touch Logica's own variables. After rebuilding, bump `CACHE` in `sw.js`.

## Animations

Poka walks, wobbles, bumps into rocks and dances at the flag, gems fly up when collected, the blocks light up as they run (inside repeats and the function too), the machine's gears spin while items feed in and shoot out, the balances tilt with a bounce, answers fly into place and matching items drop through the filter. With "reduce motion" turned on in the device settings, all animation is off.

## Content

Robot and fixing levels, for each difficulty, and Poka's messages live in the `<script type="application/json" id="content">` block in `index.html`. Maps use `.` grass, `#` rock, `~` water, `*` gem, `y` yellow tile, `R` Poka and `F` the flag. A program is written with `U D L R`; `4(RD)` repeats `R D` four times, `{F}(RD)` repeats `R D` until Poka is at the flag and `{2}(R)` until two gems are collected, `?DR` goes down on a yellow tile and right anywhere else, and `F` calls the function, which is written after `|` (`FFF|RRU`). `tools` lists the blocks a level offers besides the arrows (`loop`, `until`, `if`, `fn`) and `max` is the most blocks a program may use. Pattern, machine, balance and filter questions are generated each time.

After changing a level, check that every map can be solved:

```bash
node tools/check-levels.mjs
```

It runs each solution, checks the block limit and the blocks the level offers, checks that the limit cannot be met with arrows alone, and for levels without loops, ifs or functions checks that no shorter path exists. `--solve` also prints a shortest path for every map.

## Sound

There is no voice. Sound effects (taps, steps, bumps, gems, the machine, right and wrong answers) are made with the Web Audio API.

After changing the page, bump `CACHE` in `sw.js` so installed copies refresh.

## Offline and install

`sw.js` precaches the page and icons, and keeps Google Fonts in a separate cache. The manifest lets the game be installed to the home screen.
